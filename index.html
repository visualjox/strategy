Inside the Lab:
How I'd Own Hip-Hop
Culture if I Ran Gully Gang

How Gully Gang could engineer a cultural moment instead of selling merch.

Vishal Joshi Brand Strategy 7 min read
Album art · 1×1

There's a kid in Dharavi who knows every word of Mere Gully Mein.

He doesn't own a Divine t-shirt. And he never will.

Not because he can't afford it. Because a ₹500 t-shirt doesn't feel like Divine. And somewhere, deep down, even Divine knows this.

That's the insight. And that's where this entire strategy is built from.

Gully Labs — 1×1

The Problem Nobody's
Talking About

Divine wears Amiri. He wears Gucci. He built a brand on scarcity, struggle, and street credibility.

And then Gully Gang sells a ₹1000 graphic tee.

That's not a merch strategy. That's brand dilution dressed up as a revenue stream. Every cheap drop chips away at the mythology Divine spent years building.

The Play

We don't go to Nike. We don't go to Adidas. We go to Gully Labs.

Baaz. Divine's most iconic record. Baazigar. The mythology he built his entire identity around.

This isn't a collab. This is a convergence. Two brands built from the streets, for the streets. Gully Gang x Gully Labs isn't a partnership you pitch in a boardroom. It's a story that writes itself.

  • —No announcement. The collab is never officially confirmed until the shoe drops.
  • —The name stays Baaz. The shoe carries Divine's DNA in every design detail.
  • —Every element connects to a chapter of Nange Pair — the documentary built in parallel.
  • —Positioned as convergence, not commerce. Not "Divine drops a shoe." But "Baaz found its voice."

Before the Shoe,
the Man

We launch a documentary series. Nange Pair.

  • —Episode 1: The streets he ran on barefoot
  • —Episode 2: The first time he wore something that felt like him
  • —Episode 3: What it means to build something that lasts
Nange Pair — Documentary thumbnail · 16×9

And at every concert on this tour, Divine performs barefoot. No press release. No explanation. Just the image — and the internet does the rest.

Then we release 222 pairs. One date. No ads. No influencers. No restock.

The resale market becomes the PR campaign.

The Concept

Why This Works

This is what separates brand architecture from brand management. Divine already has the raw material. The street credibility. The cultural resonance. The audience that treats his story like scripture.

222 pairs. No ads. No dilution.
Just a mythology, engineered deliberately.

Rags to Riches:
How I'd Grow an Electrolyte Brand Without Spending a Single Rupee on Ads

90% of India is dehydrated. And everyone's fighting over the 10% who already know what an electrolyte is.

Vishal Joshi Brand Strategy 8 min read

Walk into any supplement store right now.

Every shelf looks the same. Packets of different colours, different names, same product. And on Instagram, every founder has a "building @bestelectrolytebrand" bio while praying they get attention faster than their competitor.

90% of India is dehydrated. And everyone's fighting over the 10% who already know what an electrolyte is.

That's not a market strategy. That's a race to the bottom.

So here's what I'd do instead.

Overcrowded supplement shelf · 1×1

Forget the shelf.
Find the sweat.

No online store. No warehousing. No shelf space. Fuck the intermediaries — they make you expensive and complacent before you've even earned the right to be either.

Here's what I'd kill immediately:

  • —Ecommerce store
  • —Warehouse costs
  • —Paid shelf space
  • —Paid ads
  • —Manufactured content

And here's what I'd replace it with:

  • —100 run club DMs
  • —A mocktail bar on race day
  • —Real people. Real sweat. Real feedback.

Because fun will always triumph function. If they can't touch it, feel it, or taste it — it doesn't exist.

Run club mid-race · 1×1
Mocktail bar at outdoor event · 1×1

The only spend?
The glass.

Because the sexier the glass, the more shareable the drink. Nobody posts a paper cup. Nobody posts a packet of powder. But a beautiful, well-designed glass with a drink that looks as good as it tastes? That's content that creates itself.

Beautiful glass with vibrant mocktail · 1×1
Someone holding glass mid-run · 1×1

Then chase the sweat
at scale.

The virality from the run clubs becomes the pitch deck. I'd tie up with:

  • —HYROX — the fastest growing fitness event format in India right now
  • —City marathons — Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore, Hyderabad
  • —Trail runs — niche, passionate, incredibly loyal communities
  • —CrossFit boxes — high spend, high trust, high word of mouth

Mocktail booths at the finish line. At the podium. At the exact moment someone just pushed their body to its limit and deserves something that actually tastes like a reward.

HYROX or marathon finish line · 1×1
Mocktail booth at sporting event · 1×1

The content
writes itself.

POV content. A colorful ingredient palette on the cart. The kind of video that gets shared in running WhatsApp groups before it hits the algorithm.

When you do something people know they could do themselves but aren't — it goes viral. Every runner watching thinks "why hasn't my run club done this?" That thought is the share.

Flat lay of electrolyte ingredients · 1×1
POV behind mocktail bar looking at runners · 1×1

And only then —
the drop.

Once the popularity is real, once the community is built, once people are asking where they can buy it — limited drop. Best mocktail recipes. Canned versions. With a waitlist.

Run club experience → Event virality → Waitlist signups → Limited canned drop → Ecom launch. Self-funded.

No ads. No investors. No manufactured hype. Just a community that built itself around a drink that was too good not to share.

Sleek electrolyte can — limited edition · 1×1
Waitlist landing page mockup · 1×1

While everyone else is burning money on ads and praying for ROAS, I've built a brand that people discovered through experience, remembered through taste, and shared because the glass was too good not to.
